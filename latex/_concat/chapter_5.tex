\section{Insights from Work Undertaken} \label{section:insights_from_work}

The carbon dataset exhibited strong performance when $C$ appeared in the upper slab ($C|X$), with rank correlation $\rho \approx 0.95$ and low absolute error~\nocite{}.
However, only three $C|Si$ structures with carbon in the lower slab were usable, and these performed poorly, yielding negative rank correlation~\nocite{}.
As such, carbon results are informative but limited in generalisability~\nocite{}.

Across all iterations, silicon-rich systems consistently showed poor agreement between MLP and DFT, particularly when Si was present in the lower slab~\nocite{}.
These failures were not isolated cases but appeared systematic, pointing to intrinsic limitations in the current MACE model for accurately representing Si bonding under strain or geometric asymmetry~\nocite{}.

$Ge$ and $Sn$ systems, by contrast, showed robust agreement across all ranking metrics~\nocite{}.
These elements are likely well-covered in the MACE training distribution and may benefit from their more metallic bonding character, which simplifies energy regression~\nocite{}.

\newpage Notably, $Si|Ge$ alloy systems exhibited near-perfect rank preservation, with both Spearman and Kendall values approaching $1.0$~\nocite{}.
These configurations involved uniform chemical environments and minimal interfacial disruption, suggesting that MLPs perform best when atomic arrangements remain close to their bulk-trained regimes~\nocite{}.

\section{Broader Lessons and Limitations} \label{section:broader_lessons_and_limitations}

MLPs appear reliable for ranking interfacial candidates when the bonding environments and geometries fall within the training domain~\nocite{}.
Screening workflows benefit most from MLPs in systems involving Ge, Sn, and well-registered C|X structures, where rank preservation is consistently strong~\nocite{}.
In contrast, strained or covalently dominated systems like Si|Ge or Si|C exhibit erratic performance, especially when interfacial bonding is asymmetric or poorly coordinated~\nocite{}.

These results highlight the need for delta-correction strategies or domain-specific retraining, particularly for silicon-rich or mixed-interface systems~\nocite{}.
Hybrid workflows that combine MLP-based pre-screening with selective DFT confirmation offer a scalable and pragmatic approach to large-scale interface discovery~\nocite{}.

This study reinforces the value of rank-based benchmarking; such as Spearman, Kendall, and Top-N overlap, over absolute energy metrics~\nocite{}.
In screening contexts, the ability to reliably identify top-performing configurations is more actionable than minimising raw energy error~\nocite{}.

\section{Concluding Remarks} \label{section:concluding_remarks}

This work demonstrates that while general-purpose MLPs like MACE can successfully preserve interface rankings in selected systems, their predictive utility is highly system- and geometry-dependent~\nocite{}.
Strong performance was observed in $Ge$, $Sn$, and $C|X$ systems with good atomic registry, as well as in chemically uniform $Si|Ge$ alloy configurations~\nocite{}.
In contrast, $Si$-rich and poorly coordinated interfaces exhibited systematic rank failures, underscoring the limits of current models in strained or covalently dominated regimes~\nocite{}.

By enabling fast, large-scale screening of interfacial structures in favourable systems, this study lays the foundation for accelerated discovery workflows~\nocite{}.
However, widespread application across diverse material combinations will require targeted retraining, delta-learning, or integrated MLP-DFT hybrid schemes~\nocite{}.
Future work should continue to refine these approaches, using rank-preservation metrics as a primary benchmark for practical utility in materials design~\nocite{}.

Despite the promising results in rank preservation for certain systems, this study has also highlighted substantial challenges in building a truly generalisable machine-learned potential for interface prediction~\nocite{}.
Limitations such as incomplete training coverage, extrapolation to novel configurations, and model underperformance in strained or covalently bonded systems underscore the need for further refinement~\nocite{}.
These risks are being actively addressed through targeted retraining, delta-learning corrections, and expanded benchmark testing, as outlined in Chapter~\ref{chapter:next_steps}.
While strong performance was observed in Ge, Sn, and alloy systems, achieving consistent accuracy across a broader range of interfaces will require careful design of training data and model architecture~\nocite{}.

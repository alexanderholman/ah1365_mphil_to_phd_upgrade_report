\section{Further Steps Towards Rank Preservation} \label{section:rank_preservation}

The ability to preserve DFT-calculated interface rankings is critical for efficient high-throughput screening of material systems. Absolute interface energies are less relevant in isolation if the relative favourability of competing configurations is misrepresented. This motivates the need for improving rank fidelity in machine-learned models such as MACE.

To address the limitations identified in Chapter~\ref{chapter:computational_investigation}, particularly in silicon-rich systems and mixed-coordination interfaces, future work will compare three MLP training strategies:

\begin{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Baseline MACE model} Trained primarily on bulk configurations. It offers good rank preservation in many systems but struggles in Si-rich or strained interfaces, where structural features deviate from the training distribution.

    \item \textbf{Interface-specific fine-tuning} Enhances the baseline model using additional interfacial configurations. This aims to reduce domain mismatch and improve ranking accuracy in chemically or structurally diverse cases.

    \item \textbf{Delta-learning correction} Learns the residual difference between MLP and DFT energy outputs (\( \Delta E = E_\mathrm{DFT} - E_\mathrm{MLP} \)). This approach enables low-cost calibration without full retraining, making it attractive for incremental improvements.

\end{enumerate}

To quantify the degree of rank preservation between MLP and DFT energy evaluations, we will use the following benchmarking metrics:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Spearman's rank correlation coefficient} (\( \rho \)): Measures how well the global ranking of structures is preserved between methods. Chapter~\ref{chapter:computational_investigation} reports near-perfect Spearman values (e.g., \( \rho = 0.9954 \)) in many systems.

    \item \textbf{Kendall's rank correlation coefficient} (\( \tau \)): Assesses the proportion of concordant versus discordant ranking pairs. It is more sensitive to small local swaps in ranking than Spearman's \( \rho \).

    \item \textbf{Top-N Overlap}: Evaluates how many of the top-ranked structures under DFT are also present in the MLP top-\( N \) list. This provides a practical indicator for screening performance.

    \item \textbf{Mean Absolute Error (MAE)} and \textbf{Root Mean Square Error (RMSE)}: These absolute error metrics quantify the average and variance-weighted energy deviation, even when rank preservation is strong.

\end{itemize}

These metrics will be applied across chemically diverse systems (C, Si, Ge, Sn), and varied geometries including different Miller planes and stacking shifts. Structural asymmetry and slab orientation (upper vs. lower slab) will also be included in the benchmarking matrix.

\paragraph{Error Scaling with System Size}

Preliminary results suggest that energy errors may scale with system size, particularly in Sn-rich heterostructures. For example, systems involving tin exhibited high RMSE values despite strong rank correlation (\( \rho \approx 0.9744 \)). This may be due to larger atomic volumes, heavier elements, or deeper potential wells that amplify energy deviations in absolute terms. However, no systematic analysis of this scaling effect has yet been performed.

A robustness study will be conducted to quantify how MAE and RMSE evolve as a function of total atom count or interface area. If a clear trend emerges, this could motivate size-normalised corrections or domain-specific energy thresholds for MLP screening.

\paragraph{Tracking Failed Calculations and Dataset Completeness}

Some interface evaluations—especially in large or strained systems—failed due to relaxation non-convergence, broken geometries, or invalid energy outputs (e.g., NaNs). These were marked as \texttt{is\_broken} and excluded from ranking analyses (see Chapter~\ref{chapter:computational_investigation}). However, no central mechanism currently exists to summarise failure rates or track completeness across chemical systems, orientations, or slab types.

Future iterations will include a completeness tracker that records:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The number of generated vs. successfully evaluated structures
    \item Breakdown by chemical system (e.g., Si|Ge, C|Sn)
    \item Breakdown by slab orientation and stacking direction
    \item Statistics on failure causes (e.g., relaxation timeout, unphysical overlaps)
\end{itemize}

This diagnostic layer will help identify whether certain systems are systematically excluded, potentially biasing the dataset or skewing model evaluation.
